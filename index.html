Guessing a movie's rating to within a point

A regression model trained on 7,919 rated Indian movies. From the cast, director, year, genre and votes, it predicts a rating that lands 0.77 points from the real score on average.

Two patterns showed up before any modelling. Movies from the 1990s and 2000s averaged around 5.3, while older films sat closer to 6.5, so release year became a feature. Family, Musical and Drama scored highest by genre (about 6.1) and Action lowest (about 5.4), a small gap that explains why genre alone predicts poorly.

How it was built

  1. Clean. Dropped movies with no rating, and turned text like "(2019)" and "109 min" into numbers.
  2. Build features. Genres became yes/no columns. Each director and actor became the average rating of their other movies, calculated on training data only so the test movies stayed unseen.
  3. Train. Split the movies 80/20, then trained a Random Forest and a Gradient Boosting model.
  4. Evaluate. Scored both on the held-out 20% and compared them with the always-guess-the-average baseline.

Limits worth knowing

  • Votes only exist after release. The version without them, which is the realistic pre-release model, explains about a third of the variation (R² 0.32).
  • Predictions cluster in the middle. Very low and very high ratings get pulled toward the average.
  • Newcomers have little history, so scores for new directors and actors are less reliable.
  • Plot, budget and marketing are missing, and adding them would likely improve accuracy, as would trying XGBoost or LightGBM.

Dataset: IMDb Movies India on Kaggle (adrianmcmahon/imdb-india-movies).
